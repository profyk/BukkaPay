## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how {{legal_name}}, trading as {{trading_name}} ("BukkaPay"), prepares for, responds to and reports personal information breaches and other security incidents. It applies to all BukkaPay employees, contractors and service providers who handle personal information or customer funds data on our behalf.

We publish it so customers, merchants and partners know what to expect if something goes wrong.

## 2. What counts as a breach

A **personal information breach** is any security incident that leads to personal information being accidentally or unlawfully accessed, acquired, disclosed, altered, lost or destroyed. Examples include:

- someone gaining access to customer accounts or our systems without authorisation;
- personal information sent to the wrong person;
- a lost or stolen device or document containing personal information;
- a service provider reporting a breach that affects our data;
- ransomware or other malware that makes data unavailable or exposes it.

A **security incident** is any event that threatens the confidentiality, integrity or availability of our systems or data, whether or not personal information is affected.

## 3. How to report a suspected breach

**Customers and members of the public:** email {{security_email}} or contact us in the app. If you think your own account has been compromised, change your password, and contact us immediately so we can lock the account.

**Staff and service providers:** report any suspected incident immediately — and in any event within **one hour** of becoming aware of it — to the incident lead and {{security_email}}. Do not investigate on your own, delete evidence, or contact affected people before the incident team decides how to respond.

Security researchers who find a vulnerability are welcome to report it to {{security_email}}. Please give us reasonable time to fix it before disclosing it publicly, and do not access, change or delete other people's data.

## 4. Our response process

**1. Detect and escalate.** The incident lead opens an incident record, notes the time we became aware, and assembles the response team (engineering, compliance, the Information Officer and, where relevant, legal advisers and affected partners).

**2. Contain.** We act immediately to stop the breach and limit harm — for example revoking credentials and sessions, isolating systems, freezing affected accounts or payments, rotating keys and asking recipients to delete misdirected information.

**3. Assess.** Within **24 hours** where possible, we assess what happened, what information and how many people are affected, whether funds are at risk, and the likely harm to the people concerned (such as fraud, identity theft, financial loss or distress). We keep assessing as facts emerge.

**4. Notify.** We notify regulators, affected people and partners as described in sections 5 and 6.

**5. Recover.** We restore systems and data from secure backups, confirm the threat has been removed, and monitor for further activity.

**6. Review.** After every significant incident we carry out a review to understand the root cause and what we will change, and we track those actions to completion.

## 5. Notifying regulators

Where the law requires it, we notify the relevant regulators as soon as reasonably possible after we become aware of a breach, and within any deadline set by law. For example:

- **South Africa:** the Information Regulator, as soon as reasonably possible after discovery (section 22 of POPIA);
- **Nigeria:** the Nigeria Data Protection Commission, within 72 hours where the breach is likely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms;
- **Kenya:** the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, within 72 hours;
- **EU / UK** (where GDPR applies): the competent supervisory authority, within 72 hours unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk.

We also notify, as our agreements and the law require, our Partner Institutions, our card issuer and the card networks for incidents involving card data, financial regulators for incidents affecting payment services, and law enforcement where a crime has been committed.

## 6. Notifying you

If a breach is likely to put you at risk, we will tell you directly — in the app, by email or SMS, or, if we cannot reach you, by a public notice — as soon as reasonably possible, unless a law enforcement or regulatory body tells us that notification would impede a criminal investigation.

Our notice will explain in clear language:

- what happened and when, as far as we know;
- what information about you was involved;
- what we have done and are doing about it;
- what you can do to protect yourself, such as changing your password or watching for suspicious messages;
- how to contact us for more information, and the identity of the unauthorised person if known.

We will never ask you for your PIN, password or one-time codes in a breach notice.

## 7. Records

We keep a register of all incidents and breaches, including those that did not need to be reported, with the facts, effects, decisions taken and the reasons for them. Records are kept for at least five years.

## 8. Prevention

We reduce the risk of breaches through encryption, least-privilege access, multi-factor authentication for staff, audit logs, row-level security on our database, regular security testing, vetting of service providers, staff training and tested backup and recovery procedures.

## 9. Responsibilities

- The **Information Officer** ({{information_officer}}) is responsible for regulatory notifications and for this policy.
- The **incident lead** coordinates the technical response.
- **All staff and contractors** must report suspected incidents immediately and cooperate with the response.

This policy is reviewed at least once a year and after any significant incident.
